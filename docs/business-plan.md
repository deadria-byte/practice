## The verdict, up front

**Conditional GO — to a paid validation sprint, not to a full launch.**

- The **market is not the problem.** ~25,749 US HVAC/plumbing establishments have 10+ employees; AKR needs 17 clients. Penetration required: under 0.2% of the qualified pool.
- The **price is not the problem.** $497/mo is 0.70% of revenue for a $1M shop, inside a trade that spends 8–12% of revenue on marketing. The fee is 5–12% of a typical target shop's total marketing budget.
- The **tool cost is not the problem.** Full stack runs ~$280–330/mo in validation and ~$1,120/mo at 25 clients (~9% of MRR). One client more than covers the lean stack.
- The **product definition is the whole game.** Sold as "reputation management" (review requests + replies + reports), $497/mo fails — the buyer can get that for $75/mo from NiceJob or may already have it inside ServiceTitan/Jobber/Housecall Pro. Sold as a **done-for-you review engine + customer-experience operations service** — workflow installed in their software, techs and CSRs trained and monitored, every review answered by a human, monthly scoreboard tied to calls, and a quarterly process fix — $497/mo sits *below* Podium's real-world cost and far below every specialist agency, with no direct SMB competitor found in the research.
- The **real risks are distribution and retention**: getting owners on calls (cold email reply rates are under 1% without a personalized audit), and keeping clients past months 4–6 when easy review gains plateau. Both risks are testable in a 90-day validation sprint for roughly **$2,000–$5,300 all-in** before any bigger commitment.

**Commit in stages: validation first, launch only on evidence (gates in Section 11).**

### 2.2 The stealth competitor: software they already own

This is the single most important competitive fact in the plan:

- **ServiceTitan Marketing Pro: Reputation** already does automated review requests at job completion, a consolidated review feed, AI response drafting, listings, and matches every review to the customer, job, and **technician**. It's an add-on on top of a platform reported at ~$245–500/tech/mo. A ServiceTitan shop with Marketing Pro is *already paying more than AKR's whole fee* for a bundle that includes reputation.
- **Housecall Pro** lists review management on **every plan including Basic ($59/mo)** **[P]**.
- **Jobber's Marketing Suite** (reviews + campaigns + referrals) is a **$99/mo add-on** **[P]**.
- **FieldEdge** has **no native reputation product** — its users must buy Birdeye/NiceJob separately. FieldEdge shops are the least cannibalized segment.

ServiceTitan's own published playbook admits where results actually come from: CSR sets the review expectation at booking + technician verbally prepares the customer at job completion + automation as backstop. **Two of the three legs are human process, not software** — and nobody sells the human legs at SMB price.

**What's left to sell (and it's plenty):**
1. **Activation** — a large share of shops own a review module that is off, misconfigured, or firing default copy at the wrong Google profile.
2. **Ownership** — a named human watching the feed daily, responding in hours, escalating negatives.
3. **Process change** — the scripts, ask-moment, missed-call recovery, and follow-up fixes that determine review quality.
4. **The FieldEdge gap** — a genuine software hole to fill.

### 2.3 Agencies

Scorpion (reported ~$3–7K/mo home services, 12-month contracts), RYNO/Blue Corona ("clients typically invest $5,000+/mo"; has added CSR coaching — the closest anyone gets to AKR's angle, at 10× the price), Hook Agency (published: Local SEO $3,750/mo; most clients $5–8K/mo). Specialist long-tail retainers start ~$1,000–3,000/mo.

AKR is **not** price-competing with agencies. The risk is different: a prospect already paying $3K+/mo says "reputation is included." The cleanest wedge is the **$1M–$5M shop with no agency at all**, plus shops resenting big-platform lock-in (90-day notices, renewal fees, account-manager churn are documented complaint patterns — AKR's month-to-month, you-own-everything stance is a real differentiator).

### 2.4 Competitive verdict

- **Software layer: saturated and getting cheaper. Do not build on it.**
- **Agency layer: expensive, disliked, entrenched at $3M+ shops.**
- **Service layer — national, remote, HVAC/plumbing-only, operator-led, ~$497/mo: no exact incumbent found.** The fight in every sales conversation is against a "free" alternative the prospect already owns. AKR's entire sale is the delta — activation, human ownership, operational fixes — and it must be *demonstrated* with pilot numbers within 60–90 days or churn will price AKR like software.

## 3. Feasibility

### 3.1 The market, honestly sized (Census County Business Patterns 2023, NAICS 238220)

The headline "111,207 HVAC/plumbing establishments" is misleading. By employee size:

| Size | Establishments | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 employees | 64,871 | 58.3% |
| 5–9 | 20,587 | 18.5% |
| 10–19 | 13,148 | 11.8% |
| 20+ | ~12,601 | 11.3% |

Plus 178,663 nonemployer businesses averaging $78K in receipts — **not prospects at any price.**

- **Core serviceable market: 25,749 establishments with 10+ employees.** At ~$235K revenue per employee (industry-derived), a 10-person shop implies ~$2.4M revenue; the average contractor runs 12 employees and ~$2.9M.
- With the upper half of the 5–9 band: ~34,000–36,000 locations. After haircuts for residential focus, independent ownership, and owner reachability: **>12,000 qualified locations.**
- **AKR needs 17. That is the whole feasibility story on market size: go.**

### 3.2 The money test at the customer's side

- Industry marketing spend: **8–12% of revenue** typical for residential contractors (ACHR News), 5–10% under $1M revenue, 10–15% above $3M (Contractor Marketing Index).
- A $1M shop's marketing wallet is ~$4,200–10,000/mo, fought over mainly by Google Ads/LSA (blended cost per lead ~$104). $497 is a small, defensible line **if the owner believes it produces calls** — which is why the scoreboard reports calls and direction requests, not star counts alone.
- Below ~$750K revenue / under 5 employees: expect price resistance and churn regardless of results. This sets the ICP floor in Section 4.

### 3.3 Private equity: real, fast, double-edged

- PE share of HVAC M&A rose from 8% of deals (2023) to 23% (2024); a record 55 PE HVAC deals in 2024 (+72% YoY, PitchBook); 77 transactions in H1 2025, 39 by PE.
- Apex Service Partners alone did ~60 add-ons in 2025, reaching ~300 businesses / ~$1.3B revenue — and gives acquired shops shared marketing and tech. **An acquired shop stops being a prospect.**
- By establishment count, consolidation touches <0.5% of locations per year — the independent pool is not vanishing. By revenue it's concentrated at $5M+.
- Two openings: (1) independents competing against PE-backed rivals with 900 reviews and a marketing team is a concrete, emotional pitch; (2) **exit value** — independents sell at roughly ~5× EBITDA vs ~18× for platforms, and a strong review/rating profile is visible diligence evidence. "We make you busier now and more sellable later" is a second value story no software vendor tells.

### 3.4 Seasonality — the calendar is part of the business model

- HVAC shoulders (Mar–May, Sep–Oct): call volume drops ~30%, cost per lead falls 20–40%. Peak (Jun–Aug, Dec–Feb): owners are buried — **worst time to sell or onboard, best time for a client's review generation** (job volume highest).
- Plumbing is materially flatter year-round (emergencies, water heaters; winter freeze spike).
- Selling windows = shoulders: owners have time, feel the slow phone, and can implement. Pitch: *"Build the review base in the shoulder so you win the peak."* A pilot completing 60–90 days before peak renews on peak results.
- A mixed HVAC + plumbing book hedges AKR's own pipeline: plumbing sells and proves in any month.

### 3.5 How this trade actually buys

- **The owner signs** (below ~$5–8M revenue there is usually no GM layer). **The office manager is the gatekeeper and daily user** — she can kill it or champion it, but rarely signs. Sell dual-track: owner gets ROI; office manager gets "this makes your life easier" plus a one-page brief to carry to the owner.
- **Referral-first culture:** 78% of HVAC contractors advertise mostly by word of mouth (Clear Seas/ACHR 2024); referral leads close at ~30–45% vs ~12% for shared marketplace leads. For AKR's own marketing this means: peer proof and referrals must be engineered early; cold outbound alone will not carry the plan.
- Owners aggregate at Service World Expo (1,000+ owners), ACCA and PHCC conferences, and in large Facebook owner communities (e.g., ServiceTitan Hacks, 9–11K members). AHR Expo skews manufacturers — weak owner density per dollar.
- **Cold email reality:** 0.45% average reply rate across 7.53M B2B sends (Belkins 2026); owners reply slightly above average (0.57%) but meetings land well under 1% of sends *unless the email carries a specific, personalized audit* — personalization is the single biggest measured lever.

### 3.6 Feasibility verdict

Market size, customer budget headroom, and channel access all clear the bar for a 17–24 client business. **The condition is the offer definition in Section 5.** Reputation-only is a $75–199 product in this trade; priced at $497 it dies within one sales cycle. The done-for-you operations service at $497 has no direct competitor found and sits below every alternative that includes a human.

| Review velocity | Behind top-3 local competitors | The audit gap Robert sells |
| Marketing state | **No agency**, or agency resented | Cleanest wedge; no "already included" objection |

### 4.2 Priority segments (in order)

1. **Dormant-module shops** — on ServiceTitan (no Marketing Pro reputation in use), Jobber (no Marketing Suite), or Housecall Pro (feature never configured). Pitch: *"You already own the tool. We activate it and run it."*
2. **FieldEdge shops** — no native reputation product at all; genuine gap to fill.
3. **No-agency independents at $1–5M** competing against a PE-backed rival in their market — the exit-value + David-vs-Goliath story lands here.

### 4.3 Disqualifiers (walk away, politely)

- Under 3.7 stars with operational complaints dominating (they need an operations fix before review generation — more requests will surface more damage; offer the quarterly process consult later, not the engine now)
- Under 5 employees / under ~$750K revenue (price resistance and churn regardless of results)
- New-construction-only or commercial-only (no consumer review flow)
- PE platform branch or franchise with HQ-controlled marketing
- Owner unreachable / everything delegated to a gatekeeper with no owner access (the sale and the implementation both die there)
- Anyone asking for review removal, gating, incentives, or fake reviews — hard no, every time (policy + FTC exposure, and it's the trust brand)

### 4.4 The two buyers in every deal

- **Owner (signer):** cares about calls, booked jobs, beating the named competitor across town, and what the business is worth. Gets the audit, the ROI math (one extra replacement job ≈ $5,000–16,000 pays the fee many times over), and the scoreboard.
- **Office manager (gatekeeper/champion, daily user):** cares about not adding work. Gets the promise that AKR *removes* work (we answer, we chase, we report) and a one-page brief written for her to hand to the owner. Every discovery process equips her instead of going around her.

### 4.5 Psychographic fit (the owner who stays)

Stays: growth-minded, already spending something on marketing, checks his own Google profile, hates the PE shop across town, thinks about what the business is worth someday. Churns: bought in a panic during slow season, expects reviews to fix a broken operation, won't give system access, won't let techs ask. The discovery call (Section 7) is designed to sort these in 20 minutes.

## 5. The offer and pricing

### 5.1 What $497/mo actually buys (the defensible definition)

**Install (setup, $995):**
- Google Business Profile audit & cleanup (categories, services, photos, hours, attributes)
- Review workflow installed inside the software they already run (FSM trigger at job completion) or in AKR's platform where there's a gap
- CSR booking script + technician ask script, trained in one session with the team
- Response system live: every review answered by a human, owner-approved voice, negatives escalated same day
- Competitor baseline: rating/velocity vs. their 3 named in-market competitors
- QR/review assets for trucks, invoices, and office

**Run (monthly):**
- Review generation monitored daily; ask-rate checked against completed jobs (the number that predicts everything)
- All reviews answered within one business day (target: same day)
- Monthly scoreboard the owner actually reads: new reviews, rating trend, review velocity vs. competitors, response time, Google profile calls & direction requests, ask-rate, one operational insight
- **Quarterly process fix:** one root-cause theme from their reviews (late arrival, price surprise, no follow-up, couldn't reach them) turned into one concrete SOP change, reviewed on a call — this is DeAdria's layer and the renewal engine

**Terms as positioning:** month-to-month after setup, client owns their profiles, data, and reviews forever, cancel anytime. Every major competitor's terms (Birdeye's 90-day notice + 8% renewal fee, Podium's 12-month non-refundable, Scorpion's 12-month) make this a headline, not fine print. The $995 setup is the churn protection instead of a contract lock-in — it covers onboarding labor and per-client texting registration (~$22.50–64 one-time [P]).

### 5.2 Price check

- $995 + $497/mo = **$6,959 in year 1** per client. 17 clients = $101,388 ARR (run-rate), ~$118K collected in a full steady year with setups.
- At $397/mo you'd need 21 clients for $100K; at $597, 14. $497 is the balance point: under Podium's real-world cost, defensible as a service, achievable client count for two part-time operators.
- Pilot structure for validation (Section 11): same price, 90 days, shoulder-timed where possible. Do not discount the monthly; a reduced setup for the first 3 pilots (in exchange for a case-study and testimonial agreement) is the only lever, decided before outreach starts.
- **Billing: push ACH** (Stripe ACH = $3.98 per $497 charge vs $14.72 by card [P]) — worth ~$268/mo at 25 clients. Setup fee may be card; recurring goes ACH.

### 5.3 Add-ons — parked until core is proven

Website build (fixed template, flat fee) + care plan, social content packs, ads management. These are expansion revenue from retained clients only. Launching with them triples the fulfillment surface before the core engine is proven — the original whiteboard's biggest structural risk.

## 6. Marketing plan (AKR's own)

### 6.1 The funnel math the plan must respect

Target steady state after pilots: **15–20 discovery calls/month** (Robert's capacity at 10–12 hrs/week) → at a 20–25% close rate, **3–4 new clients/month.**

Feeding that with cold email alone at benchmark rates (well under 1% of sends become meetings) implies 2,000–4,000+ delivered emails/month *with* personalized audits — doable on the infrastructure in Section 9, but only if the audit does the selling. Hence the channel mix below: cold outreach opens doors; Robert's calls, peer proof, and referrals close the gap benchmarks say cold email can't.

### 6.2 Channels, ranked by expected yield for this trade

1. **Personalized audit outreach (core engine).** Scrape HVAC/plumbing businesses nationally (Outscraper: rating + review count for ~$3 per 1,000 records [P]); filter to ICP; generate a one-page audit per prospect (their rating/velocity vs. 3 named local competitors, unanswered negatives, dormant-software signal). Sequence: audit email → Robert follow-up call → short personal video audit for warm responders → discovery call. The audit is the lead magnet, the credibility, and the discovery agenda in one artifact.
2. **Robert's phone follow-up.** This trade lives on phones; email-only sequences underperform. His queue system (scorecard mockup) works warm replies within minutes and follows a set cadence on audit recipients. Office-manager-first calls are expected and planned (equip the gatekeeper, Section 4.4).
3. **Owner communities.** Facebook owner groups (ServiceTitan Hacks at 9–11K members, trade-specific groups) — participate as operators, answer reputation questions, never pitch cold; owners ask for help in public there. One helpful presence + pilot case studies = inbound.
4. **Referral engineering (from client #1).** Trades talk peer-to-peer. Every pilot agreement includes an ask: one introduction to another owner (peer group, supply-house counter, association chapter). Referral leads close at multiples of cold; by client 10 this should be a top-2 channel.
5. **Partnerships (Year 1 second half).** FSM consultants/coaches, trade coaches, and Nexstar-type peer organizations live on referrals to vendors like AKR; a "we activate the module your clients ignore" offer makes AKR useful to them instead of competitive. Conference presence (Service World Expo, ACCA, PHCC) is a Year 2 decision, bought with revenue, not launch capital.
6. **AKR's own proof assets.** Sample audit, sample scoreboard, Robert's intro video, and (after pilots) named case studies with before/after velocity. Built once during validation; every channel above runs on them.

### 6.3 The message

Never "reputation management." Always the outcome in trade language:
- *"When someone's AC dies at 9pm, they call whoever Google shows them first with the reviews to trust. We build the review engine — and fix what's causing the bad ones — so that call is yours."*
- Supporting proofs: one extra replacement job pays the fee many times over; the PE shop across town is running this play with a team; month-to-month, you own everything.

### 6.4 Marketing calendar (seasonality applied to AKR itself)

- **Selling pushes:** Mar–May and Sep–Oct (HVAC shoulders) + year-round plumbing.
- **Peak months (Jun–Aug, Dec–Feb):** Robert shifts time from cold outreach to client proof capture, referrals, and community presence; DeAdria captures peak-season results stories for the next shoulder's campaign.
- **Validation timing note:** a sprint starting in a shoulder window gets owner attention; starting in peak means competing with emergency volume for every conversation.

### 6.5 Marketing budget

Included in the stack (Section 9): data ~$25–60/mo, outreach infrastructure ~$110–190/mo, verification ~$20–39/mo. Total acquisition tooling **under $300/mo even at scale** — acquisition cost in this business is Robert's *time*, not media spend. At 3–4 adds/month on ~44–52 Robert-hours/month, implied time-cost per client is ~12–15 hours of front-end time in the early months, falling as referrals mix in. That is the number to watch on his scorecard, not ad spend.

## 7. Sales process (Robert's side, operationalized)

1. **Queue discipline:** warm replies worked same-day (speed-to-lead is his phone-queue instinct applied to sales); follow-ups due today cleared before new outreach review.
2. **Discovery call (20–30 min, owner required):** walk their audit (their numbers vs. named competitors) → diagnose software state ("what happens today when a job closes?") → qualify the Section 4.5 fit (access, techs willing to ask, owner engaged) → present pilot terms → equip the office manager with the one-page brief if she's the path to the owner.
3. **No free consulting trap:** the audit gives the *what*; the *how* (scripts, activation, monitoring) is the product.
4. **Handoff gate to DeAdria (no exceptions):** signed agreement + setup paid + Google Business Profile access granted + job-completion data path confirmed (FSM trigger or customer list). No access + no data path = no start date. This gate is what keeps fulfillment promises honest.
5. **Scorecard targets (weekly, sized to 10–12 hrs):** ~5 discovery calls, warm replies cleared daily, follow-up cadence 100%, 8–10 video audits to top prospects. Milestones visible: first pilot → 5 clients → $5K MRR → 17 clients / $100K line. (Full mockup delivered with this plan.)

## 8. Operations (DeAdria's side)

### 8.1 The delivery stack (researched, Section 9 for costs)

- **GoHighLevel** — client sub-accounts (review monitoring/requests where the client's own FSM has a gap, response inbox, monthly scoreboard reporting), plus AKR's own CRM/pipeline, calendars, and website. Flat pricing is why the margin works: ~$12/client at 25 clients vs. NiceJob Pro's $125/client forever.
- **Client's own FSM first** — where ServiceTitan/Jobber/Housecall Pro already has the review trigger, AKR configures and monitors *that* (activation pitch) and uses its platform for reporting/response oversight. Never sell a client a second tool to do what their first tool does.
- **Outreach stack** — Smartlead or Instantly on secondary domains only (never the main AKR domain), Mailforge mailboxes, MillionVerifier verification, Outscraper data. Compliant setup: SPF/DKIM/DMARC, 2–3 weeks warm-up, 15–25 sends/mailbox/day, CAN-SPAM address + unsubscribe on every send.
- **Compliance rails (non-negotiable, in the service agreement):** ask *every* customer the same way; no review gating (Google prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews); no incentives for reviews; no fake reviews; dispute only policy-violating reviews; SMS only with documented consent (A2P registration per client). This is both legal hygiene and the trust brand.

### 8.2 Monthly operating rhythm per client (designed to be mostly system, exception-based human)

| Rhythm | Work | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Daily (batched, ~15 min total across clients) | Review feed check; negatives escalated; response drafts approved/sent | System flags → DeAdria |
| At job completion (automatic) | Review request fires from FSM/platform trigger; ask-rate vs. completed jobs monitored | System; DeAdria audits weekly |
| Weekly | Ask-rate exceptions (techs/crews not asking), unanswered-review sweep, GBP changes | DeAdria |
| Monthly | Scoreboard delivered (auto-built, human insight line added); one operational insight per client | DeAdria |
| Quarterly | Process-fix call: one root-cause theme → one SOP change; competitor re-benchmark; next quarter's target | DeAdria + owner |

### 8.3 Capacity — the honest ceiling

Time per client per month is the business's true cost driver (tools are ~$30/client):

| Ops time per client | 17 clients ($100K line) | 25 clients |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 hr/mo (heavy automation) | ~4 hrs/week | ~6 hrs/week |
| 1.5 hrs/mo (planning case) | ~6 hrs/week | ~9 hrs/week |
| 2.0 hrs/mo (hands-on) | ~8 hrs/week | ~12 hrs/week |

Alongside a W2, **~17–20 clients is DeAdria's comfortable solo ceiling** in the planning case. Beyond ~20 clients, budget a part-time VA/ops assistant (~$500/mo [E], modeled in Section 9 from client 15) for response drafting and report assembly under DeAdria's review. That is a good problem: it arrives at ~$10K MRR. Robert's ceiling binds later: at 3–4 adds/mo he has intake capacity to spare; churn replacement at 25 clients and 5% churn is ~1.3 clients/mo, well inside his funnel.

### 8.4 Roles, cleanly

- **Robert owns:** pipeline, calls, follow-up, video audits, closing, onboarding handoff *to the gate* (Section 7.5). His scorecard measures what he controls.
- **DeAdria owns:** stack build, onboarding past the gate, delivery system, client scoreboards, quarterly strategy, process fixes, numbers. Her client dashboard is the product clients see monthly (mockup delivered with this plan).
- **Weekly 1-hour joint review:** pipeline (his board) + client health (her board) + one decision. This meeting is the company's operating system.

| Lifetime value at 5% monthly churn (20-mo avg life) | ~$10,900 gross / ~$9,200 contribution |
| Stack break-even | **1 client** covers the lean stack; ~2.5 clients cover the full scale stack |

Tools are ~9% of MRR at 25 clients. **Labor — yours — is the real cost, which is why Section 8.3's ceiling matters more than any line above.**

### 9.4 Scenarios (modeled, not promised)

Assumptions: $995 setup + $497/mo; contribution costs as above; VA step +$500/mo from 15 clients; churn as stated. "Net" = after tool/payment/VA costs, **before** owners' pay, taxes, and one-time startup capital.

| Scenario | Adds/mo (after pilot) | Churn | Clients at mo 12 | Year-1 revenue | $100K run-rate reached | $100K collected reached |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Slow** | 1 → 2 | 6% | 13 | ~$55K | Month 16 | Month 17 |
| **Base** | 2 → 3 | 5% | 23 | ~$96K | Month 9 | Month 13 |
| **Fast** | 3 → 4 | 4% | 34 | ~$142K | Month 7 | Month 10 |

Read these honestly:
- **Plan on Slow, aim for Base.** Slow still reaches the $100K run-rate inside 18 months with no heroic assumptions — it only needs Robert to average ~1.5 closes/month after pilots.
- Base and Fast exceed DeAdria's solo ops ceiling (Section 8.3) during Year 1 — they *require* the VA step and tighter automation, and Fast requires Robert closing 4/mo sustained, the top of his hours. Treat them as capacity-planning cases, not forecasts.
- Every scenario is cash-positive on tools from the first client; the real investment is ~90 days of both your time before proof. That is what the validation gates protect.

### 9.5 Total capital at risk to a decision

**Validation (90 days): ~$2,000–$5,300** — one-time items in 9.1 (legal can be staged: pilot agreement first, full agreement at launch) + ~3 months of lean stack (~$840–990). If validation fails its gates, that's the loss, plus time. No long-term contracts in the stack (GoHighLevel, NiceJob-layer tools, sequencers are month-to-month) — the business can be switched off for the cost of one month's stack.

## 11. Risks, kill criteria, and the validation gates

### 11.1 Top risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation built into this plan |
|---|---|
| "My software already does reviews" | Activation pitch; sell ownership + process, never the tool (Sections 2.2, 5.1) |
| Cold outreach underperforms benchmarks | Audit-led personalization; phone follow-up; community + referral channels from client #1 (Section 6) |
| Churn at months 4–6 when velocity gains plateau | Quarterly process fix + scoreboard tied to calls; setup fee aligns commitment; shoulder→peak proof arc (Sections 3.4, 5) |
| Clients won't give access / techs won't ask | Hard onboarding gate; ask-rate monitored weekly; discovery qualifies owner engagement (Sections 4.5, 7) |
| DeAdria's time ceiling | Automation-first delivery; VA step at ~15–20 clients; client cap is a feature, not a failure (Section 8.3) |
| Compliance incident (gating, incentives, SMS consent) | Rails in the agreement + stack; hard-no disqualifier in sales (Sections 4.3, 8.1) |
| PE buys a client / client gets an agency | Expected background churn (~5% assumption already includes it); exit-value framing can delay it |
| Robert's hours shrink | The funnel is system-fed; at 5 hrs/week the model slows to Slow-case pace, it doesn't break |

### 11.2 The validation sprint (the actual next step, if you choose it)

- **Days 1–14:** Build stack (GHL + outreach on secondary domains, warm-up starts), audit template, sample scoreboard, Robert's intro video, pilot agreement. Build list: 300 ICP businesses nationally with audits generated.
- **Days 15–45:** Work 100 prospects properly (audit email + Robert's calls + video audits to warm responders). Plumbing-weighted if the window is an HVAC peak; HVAC-weighted in shoulders.
- **Days 46–90:** Discovery calls → target **3 paid pilots** at full pilot terms; deliver; measure review velocity, ask-rate, response time against each pilot's baseline.

### 11.3 Decision gates (decide these numbers now, not after you see results)

- **Gate 1 (demand):** 100 well-worked prospects → **≥10 discovery calls.** Fail = the offer or ICP is wrong; change one variable and retest once, or stop. Do not buy more tools or widen the vertical to mask it.
- **Gate 2 (willingness to pay):** ≥10 calls → **≥3 paid pilots at $497/mo** (setup may be reduced for pilots only by the pre-agreed amount). Fail = price/product-definition problem; do not discount the monthly to rescue it.
- **Gate 3 (delivery proof):** pilots show measurable review-velocity improvement and ≥80% ask-rate compliance within 90 days. Fail = the delivery model, not the market, needs the fix before any scale.
- **Full commit** only after all three gates pass. Passing all three means you have a business with paying proof; failing any one means the total loss is the Section 9.5 validation budget.

## 12. Recommendation

1. **Commit to the validation sprint, not yet to the business.** The research clears market, price, competition-gap, and cost. It cannot clear demand and retention — only 100 real prospects and 3 real pilots can.
2. **Sell the operations service, never the software.** Every artifact — audit, scorecard, dashboard, scripts — should say "review engine + we fix why the bad ones happen."
3. **Approve before the sprint starts:** pilot terms (setup treatment for the first 3), the Gate 1–3 numbers above, and the sprint start window against the HVAC calendar (shoulder preferred for HVAC weight; plumbing can start anytime).
4. **Keep add-ons parked** until 10 retained clients. The core engine is the business; everything else is expansion.
